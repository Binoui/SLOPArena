# Cartoon deformation study for Manki

Codex-prepared study, 15 October 2026. The animation observations below use the three Japanese YouTube transcripts supplied by the project owner. They are paraphrases of automatic captions, not a claim of complete visual playback review. The implementation findings come from project source inspection. No animation, gameplay values, catalog bindings, or cooked packages were changed.

## Lessons from the three references

**The Perils of Interpolation — preserve contrast between poses.** Sakurai compares kicks with the same eight-frame startup but very different impressions of speed (about 0:55–1:27). Matching the key poses and total duration is insufficient: interpolation can spend too much time in weak intermediate poses. His proposed approach gives anticipation and the attack pose more exposure, with very little time crossing between them (1:38–1:55). In the uppercut discussion, he describes pulling the load farther back, passing through intermediate poses in individual frames, and giving the follow-through its own trajectory (2:36–3:36). He also qualifies the advice: skipping motion is not automatically suitable for every game (3:39–4:06). Source: [The Perils of Interpolation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oFwamE6Hy04&t=55s).

Application to Manki: preserve impact tick 23 while concentrating the final downward swing into a short interval immediately before it. A longer-held overhead load and a decisive change to the strike can make the existing clock feel stronger. Smoothing every transition would work against that goal. Recovery can use a different arc instead of simply reversing the swing.

**Exaggerate to Make Up for Information Loss — judge the character at gameplay size.** Sakurai recounts the reasoning behind the exaggerated performances in Clock Tower 3: a polygonal character loses information carried by a live performer, so its acting needs to compensate (0:23–1:18). He then identifies character size and camera distance as further losses: motion that reads in a close-up can become understated when the character is small (1:20–1:44). A convincing sword action requires more than the literal sword swing (1:47–2:16). Source: [Exaggerate to Make Up for Information Loss](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ivwt37x-2EU&t=33s).

Application to Manki: larger fists are one way to strengthen the strike's silhouette. Torso compression, an overhead load, clear elbow separation and a directional follow-through can support the same idea. Evaluate those choices at normal gameplay camera size and speed; a close-up alone cannot establish readability.

**Too Much is Just Right — allow purposeful distortion and overshoot.** Sakurai uses Kazuya's extreme body twist to challenge judging animation only by anatomical correctness in a paused model (0:04–0:44). He describes the limitations of an older rig that allowed upper-arm rotation without shoulder-root translation, and the expressive flexibility available with newer rigs (1:07–1:41). He warns that correcting every frozen-frame deformation can remove energy (1:41–2:13). He also gives a Kirby example of moving beyond a target and settling back (2:25–2:41). Source: [Too Much is Just Right](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zNBKzLzDKtM&t=4s).

Application to Manki: temporary non-anatomical proportions can be intentional animation. The criterion is whether the distortion makes the force, direction and silhouette more legible in motion. The earlier leg repair addressed a bend direction that the owner found wrong; it does not establish a realism requirement for future poses. A brief fist-size overshoot followed by a slightly smaller enlarged hold is a possible use of the settling principle.

These are artistic applications of the references, not parameters prescribed by Sakurai. The transcripts do not specify a hand-scale multiplier or a Unity implementation.

## Proposed bounded Manki test

Keep the accepted leg-corrected candidate as the baseline and create a separate big-fist variant. Start with hand-scale curves in the animation so their relationship to the pose can be reviewed directly. This is a proposal only.

- Compare the baseline with moderate and bold enlargement, for example 1.5× and 2× hand scale. Those are test suggestions, not agreed production values.
- Keep the hands at their normal scale on entry. Enlarge them near the end of anticipation, reach the largest silhouette at impact tick 23, keep them visibly enlarged through active ticks 23–30, then settle back during recovery. A brief scale overshoot can peak at contact and settle to a smaller enlarged size during the active interval.
- First change only the hand scales, preserving the existing wrist trajectories and accepted body/leg motion. Test more extreme shoulder/body posing as a subsequent comparison so its effect remains distinguishable.
- Preserve duration 62, IASA/handoff 57, landing lag 12, entry/handoff poses and the existing root policy. Sharper spacing must fit those values.
- Inspect actual-prefab playback for wrist collapse, finger inheritance, fist overlap and transitions. Judge readability at gameplay size, with additional facing angles where an apparent improvement depends on one camera.
- If the variant is later bound and cooked, compare sampled hand positions and the actual hitbox/hurtbox overlays. Decide collision size from that evidence rather than assuming visual hand scale propagates to radii.

For GenRA, the useful next direction is explicit control over exposed poses, transition intervals and permitted deformation channels. The saved Manki generation request explicitly froze all joint translations and scales; these were experiment guardrails, not a demonstrated GenRA limitation. A deformation test would need narrow exceptions for the chosen hand-scale channels. The existing [authoring workflow](../../skills/sloparena-animation-authoring/SKILL.md#authoring-passes) already separates blocking, breakdowns, interpolation and polish. A stronger pose should survive those later passes. A simple authored scale curve would not require another generation job.

## What the current implementation actually does

**The user is right that animation can affect gameplay hitbox positions. The earlier statement that animation enlargement cannot affect gameplay was too broad. However, a larger-looking hand does not automatically produce a larger collision radius in the current implementation.** SlopArena cooks selected bone positions from the bound animation, then Shared resolves authored collision volumes around those points. It does not carry per-bone rotation or scale into the pose payload. Sources: [pose sampling](../../client/Unity/Assets/Scripts/Editor/DeterministicPoseTrackBaker.cs#L156), [pose payload](../../src/Shared/BakedAnimationData.cs#L20), [hitbox position resolution](../../src/Shared/HitboxGeometry.cs#L79), [authored radius assignment](../../src/Shared/Abilities/ServerAbility.cs#L179).

The verified package path is:

1. `character.json` chooses a semantic animation ID; `CharacterAssetCatalog.asset` binds that ID to an exact clip and rig. The cook reads the binding, uses `ceil(clip.length × 60)` frames, and samples the catalog rig at 60 Hz. Sources: [binding and frame count](../../client/Unity/Assets/Scripts/Editor/UnityCharacterAssetCooker.cs#L133), [rig passed into baker](../../client/Unity/Assets/Scripts/Editor/UnityCharacterAssetCooker.cs#L166).
2. The baker clones that rig and resolves nine Humanoid points: head, upper chest, hips, both hands, both feet, and both toes. For ordinary attack clips, each sample is the evaluated bone's world position minus the evaluated hips position. Optional weapon markers add two synthetic points. Neither skin vertices nor bone matrices, rotations, or scales are serialized. Sources: [sampled bone set](../../client/Unity/Assets/Scripts/Editor/DeterministicPoseTrackBaker.cs#L12), [clone and Humanoid lookup](../../client/Unity/Assets/Scripts/Editor/DeterministicPoseTrackBaker.cs#L62), [sample and write](../../client/Unity/Assets/Scripts/Editor/DeterministicPoseTrackBaker.cs#L156).
3. The package loader verifies the payload and maps pose-track names back to semantic animation IDs. Match catalog entries clone the definitions and baked positions; Unity-rendered transforms are not the source of runtime collision positions. Sources: [verified loading](../../src/Shared/CookedCharacterPackageLoader.cs#L78), [pose loading and ID mapping](../../src/Shared/CookedCharacterPackageLoader.cs#L108), [catalog copy](../../src/Shared/MatchContentCatalog.cs#L61), [deep copy of pose arrays](../../src/Shared/MatchContentCatalog.cs#L242).
4. Shared selects a baked frame, multiplies ordinary bone coordinates by the character's constant `HurtboxBoneScale`, and applies simulation position/facing. Hitbox offsets are in simulation meters and rotate with character facing, **not the wrist's animated orientation or scale**. A second anchor can determine a capsule's endpoint. Active bone-attached hitboxes re-resolve their positions each tick. Sources: [frame and world projection](../../src/Shared/HitboxGeometry.cs#L161), [offset and endpoint formulas](../../src/Shared/HitboxGeometry.cs#L79), [per-tick tracking](../../src/Shared/SpellResolver.cs#L235).
5. Hitbox radius is copied from authored hitbox data. Bone hurtboxes similarly follow baked points, use facing-relative offsets, and keep the authored sphere radius. `HurtboxBoneScale` scales coordinates, not those radii. Sources: [hitbox radius](../../src/Shared/Abilities/ServerAbility.cs#L179), [hurtbox construction](../../src/Shared/ServerSimulation.cs#L474).

Some comments call hurtbox offsets “bone-local”; the implementation and tests explicitly apply facing-relative offsets. The formulas above are the observed behavior. Sources: [field comment](../../src/Shared/HurtboxBoneDef.cs#L12), [actual formula](../../src/Shared/ServerSimulation.cs#L483), [facing-offset test](../../tests/Shared.Tests/HurtboxPoseTests.cs#L221).

## Consequences for deformation

The following are deductions from the verified sampling/resolution code, **not results of a scale experiment**:

- Scaling a hand about an unchanged wrist pivot can enlarge its skinned mesh and move finger descendants while leaving the sampled hand origin unchanged. The current baker does not sample fingers. In that case the hand's gameplay anchor, authored hitbox radius, and authored hand-hurtbox radius stay unchanged after cooking.
- Scaling/stretching an ancestor, or otherwise moving the wrist with the pose, can change the sampled hand position. After a new cook, the authoritative hitbox and hand hurtbox follow that changed position. If two sampled anchors separate, a capsule spanning them can become longer; its radius still does not grow automatically.
- A mesh-only deformation, such as a blend shape, does not itself enter this position-only payload. An animation-side deformation matters to collision only through changes to sampled points or separately authored collision data.
- A Unity runtime presentation effect that scales the rendered hand after the package has been cooked does not update the Shared pose samples. The same is true of changing a clip in source without regenerating/admitting the corresponding cooked data.

Unity import and actual-prefab evaluation remain a necessary check: this trace does not prove that a particular Humanoid clip will preserve proposed scale curves or that Manki's wrist weights will deform well. The existing [animation authoring workflow](../../skills/sloparena-animation-authoring/SKILL.md#unity-import-gate) requires those checks.

Weapon points are a separate existing special case: an attachment position uses `TransformPoint`, while blade endpoint offsets use the sampled weapon rotation and prefab scale. Weapon-anchored hitboxes bypass `HurtboxBoneScale`. Do not generalize that special case to Manki's fist sphere. Sources: [weapon sampling](../../client/Unity/Assets/Scripts/Editor/DeterministicPoseTrackBaker.cs#L168), [special resolution scale](../../src/Shared/HitboxGeometry.cs#L66).

## Manki's current air.4 contract

- The bound rig is `Presentation/MankiVisualRig.prefab`; `anim.manki.a4` currently binds `Art/Characters/manki/Animations/fightguy_a_4.fbx`, not the separate GenRA candidate. Sources: [rig binding](../../client/Unity/Assets/CharacterPackages/manki/CharacterAssetCatalog.asset#L17), [rig GUID](../../client/Unity/Assets/CharacterPackages/manki/Presentation/MankiVisualRig.prefab.meta#L2), [clip binding](../../client/Unity/Assets/CharacterPackages/manki/CharacterAssetCatalog.asset#L105), clip GUID (`client/Unity/Assets/Art/Characters/manki/Animations/fightguy_a_4.fbx.meta:2`, local imported asset).
- `air.4` lasts **62 ticks**, with **IASA 57**, **landing lag 12**, and `anim.manki.a4`. It spawns **one right-hand sphere**, radius **0.5 m**, offset **0.24 m forward**, at **tick 23** for **8 ticks** (23–30). It does not currently spawn one hitbox per fist. Source: [air.4 authoring data](../../client/Unity/Assets/CharacterPackages/manki/character.json#L975).
- Both hand hurtboxes have **0.12 m** authored radii and zero offsets. Manki's constant bone-coordinate scale is **0.85**. Sources: [hand hurtboxes](../../client/Unity/Assets/CharacterPackages/manki/character.json#L150), [presentation scale](../../client/Unity/Assets/CharacterPackages/manki/character.json#L45).

For a future bounded big-fist test, preserve this timing first, evaluate the imported scale curves on the actual rig, and compare the cooked anchors plus authored collision volumes. A decision to enlarge the 0.5 m hitbox or 0.12 m hurtboxes would be a distinct gameplay change. This is a proposed validation approach, not an implementation performed by this study.

## Evidence and limits

The animation study is transcript-based; automatic-caption errors make it unsuitable for exact quotations or a frame-by-frame claim. The technical investigation inspected source only; no Unity operations or builds/tests were run. Existing coverage checks per-tick bone tracking, facing-relative offsets, fixed hurtbox radii, and weapon-anchor scale handling: [tracking](../../tests/Shared.Tests/BoneHitboxTrackingTests.cs#L13), [hitbox offset](../../tests/Shared.Tests/HitboxGeometryTests.cs#L147), [Manki hurtbox radius](../../tests/Shared.Tests/HurtboxPoseTests.cs#L62), [weapon scale exception](../../tests/Shared.Tests/HitboxGeometryTests.cs#L257). Those tests were read, not rerun, and do not establish a hand-scale import/deformation result.
