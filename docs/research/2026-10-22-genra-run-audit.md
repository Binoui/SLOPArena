# Recent GenRA animation runs: timing, outcomes and next improvements

Prepared 22 October 2026 from local run artifacts, provider event streams, Unity review evidence, owner feedback and the current toolkit. **Accounting cutoff: 10:05 Europe/Paris.** No generation or paid review was started for this audit. Two existing Bonk revisions were still running at the cutoff.

The pipeline is producing useful, owner-approved animation. Bashie's idle and run are the strongest recent example: two original clips, one corrective pose pass, $5.16 of recorded model usage, and acceptance on the actual character in game. The expensive cases are constrained prop interactions, repeated geometric checks and revisions that fight the approved poses. Improving those should come before changing the authoring model.

**What the numbers mean.** Dollar amounts are the CLI's recorded API list-price equivalents under the saved Claude login, not subscription invoices. Times below sum `summary.json.wallSeconds`: time inside each authoring CLI job, including its tools. They exclude the runner's subsequent export/render work, Unity coordination, review evidence preparation, owner waiting and integration. Jobs can overlap, so summed job time is not elapsed project time. Automated review charges are included separately where recorded. Generated concept images, video references, rig construction and the surrounding assistant sessions are outside this ledger.

The primary window contains **16 ended authoring attempts, including one timeout, and four unique automated reviews**. Recorded cost is **$17.81 plus the unreported timeout cost**; summed authoring time is **3 h 1 min**. This excludes the two active revisions. Prepared directories without a provider stream are not counted as paid attempts. Copied reviews and copied/chained key runs are deduplicated.

Its next motion take took 20m 17s and $1.87; it passed numeric checks but the review found an awkward repeated plank motion during recovery. The latest take took 33m 06s and $2.86 before review. **The highest-value improvements, in order.**

1. **Use the same constraint set for authoring, review and final selection.** This is a confirmed correctness gap. The Joust gallop revision's authoring report says FAIL because approved poses moved by 24 cm, but its review report says PASS. `run.author()` adds `approved_keys` to its resolved scratch brief; `review.evidence()` reconstructs the original authoring brief, which lacks them. `loop.main()` then uses that review PASS for its stopping/fallback logic. The current fallback therefore cannot reliably protect approved poses. Pass the resolved contract through review and combine its hard result with the authoring result before selecting a take. This is our integration bug, not an upstream GenRA defect. Evidence: author check at `artifacts/genra-runs/20261021-203551-bonk-sir-r-gallop-loop202827-r1/check.txt`, review check at `artifacts/genra-loops/20261021-202827-bonk-sir-r-gallop/review-1/checks.txt`, [review implementation](../../tools/genra/review.py), [loop](../../tools/genra/loop.py).

2. **Make numerical iteration cheap; keep full geometry checks at delivery.** The latest Barn Door authoring job reports 10m 22s of API time, while timestamped tool spans total 22m 44s of its 33m 06s wall time. Those are elapsed tool spans, not CPU measurements. A separate read-only profile on its saved 94-sample, 68-joint result took **31.5s for one full check**: 17.0s in face visibility and 14.0s in mesh clearances. Visibility drew about 10.3 million triangles through Pillow. The same profiled check with only visibility and clearance disabled took **0.485s**, retaining numeric anatomy, timing, grip and key checks. Use a small explicit iteration mode, run relevant geometric checks after meaningful pose changes, and run the complete check before delivery. Avoid default sheet/motion rendering on every numerical attempt. This is a measured check-level opportunity, not a demonstrated 65-fold whole-pipeline speedup. Halving all local tool time in that job would save roughly 11 minutes if everything else stayed equal. Full profile at `tmp/genra-audit-20261022/profile-check.txt`, numeric profile at `tmp/genra-audit-20261022/profile-check-numeric.txt`.

3. **Preserve approved intent without freezing every body part on every story tick.** Keep hard contact/release ticks, feet, grips, root policy and the accepted silhouette. Distinguish them from secondary head/spine motion and arrival timing that may move within an agreed envelope. The Q and Barn Door replies explicitly describe reverting overlap to satisfy the complete pose lock. Before spending on smoothing, check whether the requested change is feasible under that lock. Do not silently loosen owner-approved poses; make the proposed freedom concrete in the candidate. This needs a small approved contract change before implementation.

4. **Repair a diagnosed interval and preserve the last acceptable take.** Joust gallop r0 took 5m 02s and $0.63; its attempted revision took 14m 38s and $2.75, moved approved poses 24 cm and still attracted major review notes. The owner retained r0. The current owner-direction and skipped-issue options are useful improvements already in place. Keep owner review between revisions, use a matching style reference, and stop once the owner accepts the intended finish level. Manki's accepted blocking is evidence against automatically polishing every warning.

5. **Freeze inputs and record complete stage timing/cost.** Runs hash the rig pack at admission, but their wrappers read the live shared toolkit; not every tool revision is captured. Bonk's mesh also changed during the work. Record the toolkit revision/diff hash, rig manifest, brief, approved keys and imported asset identity for every take. Track prepare, author, postprocess, Unity wait, Unity work, review and integration separately, including a terminal state on interruption. A timeout with a usable checkpoint and missing cost should remain visible. Do not charge copied `review-0` folders twice; do not log unknown authoring cost as `$0.00` when resuming a loop.

**Cheaper without changing the author.** The recent jobs already use Opus 5.5 medium effort and five-minute caching. Keep that default. Across the 15 primary-window jobs with complete usage, output accounts for about $6.86, cache reads $4.13 and cache writes $5.39 of the $16.38 authoring total. Fewer repair turns save all three. At the same token counts, five-minute rather than one-hour cache writes already avoid about $3.23; this comparison assumes the same cache-hit pattern. The [current Anthropic documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching) confirms the recorded $5 versus $8 per million write-token rates. An untested cheaper reviewer is a later experiment: halving Q's $0.32 review charge would save only about $0.16 on its $2.01 workflow. The [earlier provider comparison](2026-10-16-genra-opus-ab.md) supports retaining Opus for creative authorship, but is too small to establish a universal model ranking.

**What to tell the GenRA team.** This has become an applied rig-aware animation pipeline with real game integration, human direction and useful failure evidence. The October 14–16 experiments used upstream GenRA's prompt/parser/interpolation path. The latest runs instead use our local Python pose/IK toolkit and direct quaternion USD writer, followed by Unity Humanoid conversion; the current path does not invoke upstream `anim_gen.generate_animation`. Label those routes accurately. The [upstream pipeline description](https://github.com/MacPaw/GenRA#pipeline) is useful context, but our latest timings are not an unmodified GenRA benchmark.

The [team-report draft](2026-10-22-genra-team-report-draft.md) focuses on transferable needs: a supported agent/tool adapter, anatomy and Humanoid constraints, scoped refinement, two-handed props, reproducible validation and stage-level observability. It distinguishes our bugs from requests for upstream support. It has not been sent.

**Implementation follow-up, 22 October.** The owner authorized items 1 and 2. Runs now keep a usable resolved brief and approved-key snapshot outside writable scratch. Review uses that contract; reused reviews rerun full checks. Final selection requires passing full authoring and review reports and reports no passing candidate if every round fails. Rechecking the saved Joust clips reproduced round 1's 23.95 cm approved-pose failure and selected round 0.

The new `genra check --fast` / `check_clip(..., mode='fast')` skips mesh clearance, face visibility and the gait's mesh arm-gap check, with no automatic CLI renders. Full checks remain the default and run at review and delivery. On the saved Barn Door clip, one unprofiled check took **0.225s fast versus 25.276s full**, with identical hard failures (none) and all retained numerical metrics. Five geometry warnings were deferred; the full report still includes them. This measures check execution, excluding input loading, output renders, model calls and Unity. It does not yet measure whole-job time or cost savings. Verification record at `tmp/genra-check-fixes-20261022/verification.json`, [offline regression coverage](../../tools/genra/test_pipeline.py). All 76 toolkit tests passed; documentation checks passed.
